Things to try
  1. Watch it settle. Start with H₂ + I₂. HI climbs, then levels off. Reactions keep happening, but the forward and reverse rates match. That is equilibrium.
  2. Add a catalyst mid-run. The mix reaches its level sooner, but the final amounts barely change. A catalyst lowers the hump in both directions.
  3. Turn up the heat. Reactions speed up, because more collisions have enough energy to get over the hump. Notice the equilibrium also shifts slightly toward H₂ and I₂, since this reaction gives off energy.
  4. Start from HI only. It settles to the same mix as before. Equilibrium doesn't depend on which side you start from.
  5. Go as cold as it will go. Collisions still happen constantly, but almost none have enough energy to react.

Simplified model: a 2D box, particles drawn as discs with equal mass, and energies in made-up units. It is designed to show the ideas of collisions, activation energy, catalysts and equilibrium, not to predict real numbers.
